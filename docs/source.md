# What we learned from a year of retrospectives

When our team started doing retrospectives, the idea was simple: once every two weeks, sit down for an hour and talk about what went well and what didn't. A year later the meeting looks quiet different from how it begun, and I wanted to write down what changed and why.

## The first attempts

The first few retros where awkward. Everyone agreed that communication could be better, nobody said what they actually ment by it, and we left with a list of action items that no one looked at again. Its a familiar pattern, I think. The meeting exists, so it must be working.

What made it worse was that the same three people did most of the talking. The rest of the team was polite, nodded alot, and kept there real opinions for the coffee machine afterwards. Have to admit I was one of the talkers.

## Small changes that helped

The biggest improvement came from writing before speaking. Now everyone gets five minutes at the start to write down their points on cards, alone and in silence. Only then we read them out. This sounds like a small thing but it changed who's voice got heard, because the quiet people had already committed there thoughts to paper before the loud ones could set the agenda.

The second change was to limit ourselves to one action item per retro. One. It felt strange at first, we had so many ideas, but a single item that actually gets done is worth more then ten that get forgotten. We also started each meeting by checking the item from last time, which made it much harder to quietly drop it.

Third, we rotated who runs the meeting. The facilitator does not share their own points that session, they only make sure everyone else gets to. This was less popular, some people really do not enjoy running meetings, but it broke the pattern of the same voices dominating and it gave everyone a feel for how hard it is to keep a discussion on track.

## Where we are now

The meeting is shorter, usually fourty minutes, and people show up with things to say. Not every retro is great, some are still flat, but the average has gone up alot and the action items now gets done more often then not. If your team is stuck in the polite-nodding phase, I would start with the silent writing. It costs nothing and it is the one change I would not want to loose.
